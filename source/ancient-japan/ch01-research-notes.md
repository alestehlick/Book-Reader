# Chapter 1 research and revision notes

The supplied chapter and its accurate OCR remain the narrative base. Several chronological and population claims predate decisive revisions, so the production chapter preserves the source's evidentiary breadth while correcting the points below.

- Secure occupation is placed around 38,000 years ago, with pre-40,000 claims treated cautiously after reassessment of Japan's Early Paleolithic record. See [Ono, “Current framework and state of research on the Paleolithic in Japan”](https://doi.org/10.1537/asj.119.1) and [Naitoh et al., overview of migration models](https://doi.org/10.1537/ase.201215).
- Odai Yamamoto I pottery is placed in the closing Pleistocene. See [Taniguchi and Kawaguchi, radiocarbon study](https://doi.org/10.4116/jaqua.40.485).
- The beginning of wet-rice agriculture in northern Kyushu is moved to approximately the tenth century BC rather than the old ca. 300 BC convention. See [Sakamoto et al., tree-ring radiocarbon chronology](https://doi.org/10.1017/RDC.2023.35).
- Migration is presented as regionally variable movement and admixture, not the biological replacement of one bounded “race” by another. See [Cooke et al., ancient genomics](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abh2419) and [Mizuno et al., mitochondrial population history](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-91357-2).
- The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki are kept distinct from contemporary evidence; Chinese dynastic testimony is likewise identified as an external, court-framed archive.

The two standing chapter maps are presented without added titles or captions. Their interactive legends explain the map symbols, while the map remains visible for every paragraph.
