# Chapter 1 research and revision notes

The supplied chapter remains the narrative base, but several of its chronological
and population claims predate decisive revisions. The web edition therefore uses
the source's evidentiary breadth while correcting the points below.

- The first secure occupation is placed around 38,000 years ago, with pre-40,000
  claims treated cautiously after reassessment of Japan's Early Paleolithic record.
  See [Ono, “Current framework and state of research on the Paleolithic in Japan”](https://doi.org/10.1537/asj.119.1)
  and [Naitoh et al., overview of migration models](https://doi.org/10.1537/ase.201215).
- Odai Yamamoto I pottery is placed in the closing Pleistocene, around 16,500
  calibrated years ago. See [Taniguchi and Kawaguchi, radiocarbon study](https://doi.org/10.4116/jaqua.40.485).
- The beginning of wet-rice agriculture in northern Kyushu is moved back to about
  the tenth century BC rather than the old ca. 300 BC convention. See
  [Sakamoto et al., tree-ring radiocarbon chronology](https://doi.org/10.1017/RDC.2023.35).
- Migration is presented as regionally variable movement and admixture, not as a
  biological replacement of one bounded “race” by another. See
  [Cooke et al., ancient genomics](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abh2419) and
  [Mizuno et al., mitochondrial population history](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-91357-2).
- The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki are kept distinct from contemporary evidence; Chinese
  dynastic testimony is likewise identified as an external, court-framed archive.

The two standing maps are clean crops of Maps 1.1 and 1.2 in the supplied source.
No new title, caption, provenance panel, or other information has been added to
their presentation. Their own printed keys remain part of the historical images.
